## Brutally Honest Review (BHR)
I have to say that I quite like the idea, but also that I don't really understand the motivation behind it, nor do I think the authors really explained *why* this approach should work. My reasons for liking the JEPA appraoch are basically that it "makes sense" to do calculations in the embedded space because it matches my expectation that "thinking" occurs on a "latent" representation of the world. But I don't really have a good explanation for why this should be the case or what the mathematical advantage or reasoning for this would be. All I can say something like, "there is no reason why a thinking machine should consider most object at a minute level of detail because these details do not generally help the machine understand." Thus, even for very "complex" objects, the relevant representation has much smaller dimension than a pixel-like representation.

Self-supervised learning is an interesting area of research that seems to be broadly applicable. It is highly desirable to build models based on self-supervised learning that do not require fine-tuning for downstream tasks. There *should* be a visual model that does not need to be further trained that forms that basis of visual intelligence. Do we find that areas of the brain associated with "core" understanding exhibit less plasticity? This finding would support the idea of a number of primitive "world models" that AI relies on to relatively quickly solve more complex, multi-modal problems.
